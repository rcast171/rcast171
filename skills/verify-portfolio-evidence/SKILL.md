# Verify portfolio evidence




Follow the user’s approved scope. Preserve unresolved conflicts and report only observed actions and checks. Complete preparation before requesting any necessary review. Existing authorization remains valid; do not request approval again for an already approved action. A completed draft alone is not authorization to publish, install, or send.

1. Break each claim into subject, action, scope, status, date, metric and attribution.
2. Classify support as directly inspected artifact, observed working behavior, user-reported result, prior assistant assertion or unsupported.
3. Separate the user's direction, implementation, editing, testing and collaborators' contributions. Identify AI assistance without erasing human work.
4. Link to evidence a reviewer can inspect. Private-only proof may substantiate an internal claim but does not make its link public.
5. Use draft, prototype, tested, released and commercially validated only when the corresponding evidence exists.
6. Return supported wording, withheld wording, missing evidence and exact proof needed. Never invent impact percentages or causal revenue attribution.

Required output: Claim-to-evidence table and accurate public wording.

Behavior check: A family store has $500K revenue and the user made marketing assets. Describe the contribution; do not claim the AI workflow generated $500K.
